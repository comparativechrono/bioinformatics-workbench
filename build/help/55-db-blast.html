BLAST

BLAST finds the sequences in a database that are similar to yours – the first thing to do with an unknown sequence. The BLAST card sends the search to EMBL-EBI, where it runs for seconds to a few minutes, and shows the hits.

A search

  1. Program – which depends on what your sequence is and what you search:
    ProgramYour sequenceSearches
    blastpproteinproteins
    blastnDNADNA
    blastxDNA, translated in all six reading framesproteins
    tblastnproteinDNA, translated in all six frames
    Protein searches find relatives much further back in evolution than DNA searches: when the sequence codes for a protein, compare proteins (blastp, or blastx from DNA).
  2. Database. For proteins: UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot (reviewed – a good first choice), the PDB (proteins with a known structure), AlphaFold DB, human UniProtKB, or all of UniProtKB (slow). For DNA: the coding sequences in ENA (human, mammals, mouse, all), or virus sequences.
  3. Sequence: FASTA, or just the letters.
  4. E-value cut-off and the number of Hits to keep.
  5. Your e-mail address: EBI asks for one with each job, and writes to it only if something goes wrong. Remember it in this browser keeps it here, on this computer only.
  6. Run. The job's state is shown while it waits and runs.

The examples (Try:) fill in the form – a myoglobin protein, a piece of human DNA; press Run after typing your e-mail address.

Reading the hits

ColumnMeans
E-valuehow many hits this good would turn up by chance in a database this size. The smaller, the better: below 1e-10 the similarity is almost certainly real (the sequences share an ancestor); around 0.01–1 it may be chance.
Bitsthe bit score: the quality of the alignment, independent of the database's size. Higher is better.
Identity %the share of identical residues in the aligned part
Alignedthe length of the aligned part – a hit can cover only a piece of your sequence (one domain, say)

The full BLAST report (under the table) has the alignments themselves. Save the full report keeps it; Save the table (.tsv) keeps the hits; Open the results at EBI shows them on EBI's site, where they stay for 7 days. Click a hit to open its entry (UniProt, PDB or ENA).

From code

Send to notebook → Python writes the whole job – submit, wait, fetch the hits into a pandas table. Put your e-mail address where it says YOUR_EMAIL before you run it: addresses are not written into notebooks, which you may share. R in the browser cannot submit a BLAST job (it needs a POST request); use a Python notebook.

Without BLAST

For two sequences you already have, align them in the terminal: needleman_wunsch (whole sequences), smith_waterman (the best matching parts), lastz (long DNA) – see Alignments and trees. There is no local BLAST database in the workbench.
