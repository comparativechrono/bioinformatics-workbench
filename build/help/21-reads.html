Reads: quality, trimming, simulation

FASTQ files hold reads with a quality for every base. fastp checks and trims them, seqtk does many small jobs, wgsim makes simulated reads from a reference.

The examples work in a folder of your own:

A look at the reads

head -8 /data/examples/reads/NA12878_R1.fastq
seqtk fqchk /data/examples/reads/NA12878_R1.fastq | head -4

Each read is four lines: @name, the bases, +, and one quality letter per base (Phred+33: I = 40, 5 = 20, + = 10). seqtk fqchk summarises the bases and their qualities.

fastp: quality control and trimming

fastp -i /data/examples/reads/NA12878_R1.fastq -I /data/examples/reads/NA12878_R2.fastq \
      -o trimmed_R1.fastq -O trimmed_R2.fastq \
      --detect_adapter_for_pe --cut_tail -q 20 -l 50 \
      -h fastp.html -j fastp.json
open fastp.html

With these options fastp removes adapters, trims low-quality bases from the 3′ ends (--cut_tail), and drops reads with too many bad bases or fewer than 50 bases left; the two output files stay in step (read for read). Without --cut_tail or --cut_front fastp trims no ends: it removes adapters and drops poor reads. The HTML report shows quality, GC content and duplication before and after; fastp.json has the same numbers for a script (jq .summary fastp.json).

OptionMeans
-q 20a base is good with Phred quality ≥ 20
-u 40drop a read with more than 40 % bad bases (the default)
-l 50drop reads shorter than 50 bases after trimming
--cut_tailtrim low-quality bases from the 3′ end in a sliding window
--detect_adapter_for_pefind the adapters of paired-end reads from the reads themselves

seqtk: small jobs

# FASTQ to FASTA
seqtk seq -a /data/examples/reads/NA12878_R1.fastq > R1.fasta
# 500 random pairs: the same seed (-s) for both files keeps the pairs together
seqtk sample -s 7 /data/examples/reads/NA12878_R1.fastq 500 > sub_R1.fastq
seqtk sample -s 7 /data/examples/reads/NA12878_R2.fastq 500 > sub_R2.fastq
# length and base counts of each sequence
seqtk comp /data/examples/sequences/myoglobins.fasta | cut -f 1,2

Also: seqtk trimfq (trim by quality), seqtk subseq (pick sequences by name), seqtk rename. seqtk alone lists its commands.

wgsim: simulated reads

Simulated reads from a known sequence are a good way to test a pipeline: you know where every read belongs.

wgsim -N 2000 -1 100 -2 100 -e 0.01 -r 0.001 -S 11 /data/examples/reads/reference.fa sim_R1.fastq sim_R2.fastq
head -4 sim_R1.fastq

-N pairs, -1/-2 read lengths, -e error rate, -r mutation rate, -d/-s fragment length and spread, -S seed. A read's name says where it came from (human_CYP2C19_12642_13173_…). This build does not print the list of mutations it made.
