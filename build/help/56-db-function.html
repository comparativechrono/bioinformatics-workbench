Pathways, GO and enrichment

What does a protein do? Reactome names the pathways it takes part in, the Gene Ontology card lists the standard terms for its function, the processes it is part of and where it is in the cell, and g:Profiler finds what a whole list of genes has in common. Which proteins it works with – STRING, IntAct and NDEx – is on the page Interactions and networks.

Reactome

Reactome is a curated database of human biological pathways – reactions, the molecules in them, and how they connect – with predictions for other species. Type a UniProt accession: the answer lists the pathways the protein is in, with Diagram for those that have one; Disease marks pathways of a disease.

Gene Ontology

The Gene Ontology (GO) is a dictionary of terms that describe what genes do, so that all databases say it the same way. It has three parts (aspects):

AspectDescribesExample
Molecular functionwhat the protein does itselfGO:0003700 DNA-binding transcription factor activity
Biological processthe larger process it takes part inGO:0006915 apoptotic process
Cellular componentwhere in the cell it isGO:0005634 nucleus

GO terms of a protein (from QuickGO): type a UniProt accession, and choose an aspect or all three. Each term has an evidence code that says how it was assigned: EXP, IDA, IMP, IPI … from experiments; IBA, ISS from related genes; IEA automatically, without a curator; TAS, NAS from statements in papers. The qualifier says how the protein relates to the term (enables, involved in, located in, part of, or NOT).

Look up a term (from the Ontology Lookup Service) searches the terms of GO and other ontologies: the Human Phenotype Ontology (symptoms and signs), Mondo (diseases), Uberon (anatomy), the Cell Ontology, ChEBI (chemicals), the Sequence Ontology (the names of variant consequences used by VEP).

g:Profiler

Lists of genes – the genes changed in an experiment – are compared against GO and the pathway databases with an enrichment (over-representation) analysis: which terms occur more often in the list than among all the genes of the species? g:Profiler's g:GOSt answers for GO, KEGG, Reactome and WikiPathways at once. Type the genes (names or Ensembl IDs), choose the organism and the sources, and Search.

  • The p-values are adjusted for testing thousands of terms with g:Profiler's own method, g:SCS, made for the way GO terms overlap; terms below 0.05 are listed.
  • Genes is the number of your genes with the term, over the number of genes in the genome with it: a term with 3 of your genes out of 20 in the genome says more than 3 out of 2,000.
  • g:Profiler says which of your names it did not know – check their spelling, or use Ensembl IDs.

The test compares your list with the whole genome. If your genes could only come from a smaller set – the genes detected in your experiment – the whole genome makes many terms look enriched; on g:Profiler's own site you can give that set as the background. STRING's enrichment (on Interactions and networks) and the Networks tab's Analyse → Functional enrichment do the same test, and can use the nodes of a network as the background.

In a notebook, the recipe Databases from code asks UniProt about many proteins at once and reads the answer as a table: add go_p, go_f or go_c (biological process, molecular function, cellular component) to the fields of its query, and the table has their GO terms.
