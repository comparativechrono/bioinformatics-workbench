Expression data on an interaction network

The genes that a drug switches on or off make a list; the proteins they code for work together in a network. This recipe takes the result of the RNA-seq recipe – the genes that the steroid dexamethasone changes in airway smooth muscle cells – onto their STRING network, colours them by how much they changed, and looks for the hubs and clusters of the network and for what the clusters do. Most of it happens in the Networks tab, with the buttons below; the last steps do the same in Python.

1. The results of DESeq2

A row for each gene of the experiment: its Ensembl ID, its mean count (baseMean), how much it changed in treated cells (log2FoldChange: 1 is twice as much RNA, −1 half) and the adjusted p-value (padj). It is the file deseq2_dex_vs_untreated.csv that the RNA-seq recipe writes; a copy is in /data/examples/networks/airway_deseq2.csv.

2. From the table to a network

(if you made your own table in the RNA-seq recipe, choose it there instead). The tab finds the column of IDs and DESeq2's columns padj and log2FoldChange by their names. Keep the genes with padj < 0.01 and |log2FoldChange| ≥ 1.5 – 409 genes –, leave human, medium confidence (0.4) and all associations, and Make the network.

  • The IDs go to STRING, which finds the proteins – 357 of the 409 genes. Most of the others make no protein (long non-coding RNAs, pseudogenes), or have IDs that Ensembl has since retired: the airway data use an older annotation.
  • STRING returns the associations among the 357 proteins with a score of at least 0.4: 512 of them.
  • Every column of the table is added to the nodes – the rows are matched on the IDs that were sent, which the nodes keep in the column query.
  • The nodes are coloured by log2FoldChange – red up, blue down, grey unchanged – and sized by padj, the smallest p-values largest. The ends of the colour scale hold 95% of the genes, so that a few extreme ones do not wash out the rest (≤ and ≥ in the legend).

The finished network (the button at the top) was made in the same way on 13 October 2026, from STRING 12.5, and opens without the internet. STRING changes from version to version: made today, the network can differ a little.

4. Hubs

with MCC, Degree, Betweenness and Closeness ranks the proteins. The most connected are leptin (LEP, 26 partners), thrombospondin 1 (THBS1, 21), the chemokine CXCL12 (19) and FOXO1 (16); MCC puts CXCL12, THBS1, LEP, VCAM1 and TGFB2 first; betweenness LEP, THBS1, FOXO1, CAMK2A and GPX3 – the proteins that link the parts of the network. The hubs are a mix of genes switched on (LEP, THBS1, FOXO1) and off (CXCL12, VCAM1, TGFB2).

A hub of a network made from databases is partly a well-studied gene: leptin and the chemokines are the subject of thousands of papers, and STRING's evidence – experiments, curated databases, text mining – grows with them. A hub is a reason to look closer, not a finding on its own. (What each ranking measures: Network analysis.)

6. What the clusters do

Each cluster in the Results panel has a link enrichment: its genes go to STRING, which lists the GO terms and pathways they share more than chance would. : ECM–receptor interaction and focal adhesion (KEGG), integrin cell surface interactions (Reactome), collagen trimers (GO). Click a term to select its genes on the network. Cluster 3 gives signalling by G-protein-coupled receptors, cAMP and calcium – and, among the WikiPathways, Albuterol and budesonide therapy for asthma: a bronchodilator and a steroid, for the disease these airway cells are studied for. Cluster 4 gives the PPAR pathway and the response to fatty acids; Analyse → Functional enrichment of the whole network, among others, the regulation of lipolysis, the extracellular matrix, signalling by G-protein-coupled receptors, and cytokine–cytokine receptor interaction.

Five genes are a small sample: an enriched term among them is a hint. And the test compares the genes with the whole genome, although only genes switched on in these cells could have been found; with the box Background, STRING compares them with the network instead.

8. The same in Python

The network can be made and analysed in a notebook too, with STRING's API and networkx – useful to repeat it on other experiments, or to keep the analysis with the code of the rest of the project. The table, filtered as in step 2:

import pandas as pd, requests, time
import networkx as nx

de = pd.read_csv("/data/examples/networks/airway_deseq2.csv", index_col=0)
sig = de[(de.padj < 0.01) & (de.log2FoldChange.abs() >= 1.5)].sort_values("padj")
len(sig)

STRING's identifiers for the genes, then the network among them. STRING asks for a second's pause between requests, and for a name for the program that asks (caller_identity):

STRING = "https://string-db.org/api/json/"
me = "bioinformatics_workbench"
found = pd.DataFrame(requests.post(STRING + "get_string_ids", data={
    "identifiers": "\r".join(sig.index), "species": 9606, "limit": 1, "echo_query": 1, "caller_identity": me}).json())
time.sleep(1)
edges = pd.DataFrame(requests.post(STRING + "network", data={
    "identifiers": "\r".join(found.stringId), "species": 9606, "required_score": 400, "caller_identity": me}).json())
len(found), len(edges)

A networkx graph, with DESeq2's values on its nodes; its most connected and most central proteins:

G = nx.Graph()
for _, g in found.iterrows():
    G.add_node(g.preferredName, ensembl=g.queryItem,
               log2FoldChange=float(de.log2FoldChange[g.queryItem]), padj=float(de.padj[g.queryItem]))
G.add_weighted_edges_from(zip(edges.preferredName_A, edges.preferredName_B, edges.score), weight="score")
print(G.number_of_nodes(), "proteins,", G.number_of_edges(), "interactions,", nx.number_connected_components(G), "components")

degree = pd.Series(dict(G.degree())).sort_values(ascending=False)
betweenness = pd.Series(nx.betweenness_centrality(G, normalized=False)).sort_values(ascending=False)
pd.DataFrame({"degree": degree.head(8), "log2FoldChange": [G.nodes[n]["log2FoldChange"] for n in degree.head(8).index]})

The same numbers as the tab's – and betweenness puts LEP, THBS1 and FOXO1 first again (betweenness.head()). Louvain communities, and the network in the Networks tab:

communities = nx.community.louvain_communities(G, seed=1)
big = sorted((c for c in communities if len(c) > 1), key=len, reverse=True)
print(len(big), "communities, modularity", round(nx.community.modularity(G, communities), 3))
nx.write_graphml(G, "airway_string.graphml")
!network airway_string.graphml

networkx takes the nodes in a random order (fixed here with seed=1), the Networks tab in the order of its table: their communities are alike, but not the same. In the tab, colour the nodes again by log2FoldChange: the Style panel, Fill colour, continuous.

Good to know

STRING's associations are not all physical: two proteins of the same pathway are associated without touching. For a network of physical contacts only, choose physical interactions only in step 2, or IntAct (Interactions and networks). The gene names go to STRING; the table stays in the browser – never send personal or patient data to these services.
