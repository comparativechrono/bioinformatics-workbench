UniProt

UniProtKB, the UniProt Knowledgebase, is the reference collection of protein sequences and of what is known about each protein. The UniProt card shows its entries laid out like the pages of uniprot.org, from live data.

Reviewed and unreviewed

UniProtKB has two parts. Swiss-Prot (★ Reviewed) entries are read and written by expert curators, from the literature; there are about 575,000 of them. TrEMBL (Unreviewed) entries – about 150 million – are annotated automatically, mostly from genome sequences (numbers of release 2026_03). When a reviewed entry exists, use it.

Each entry has an accession (P04637), which never changes and is the one to cite, and an entry name (P53_HUMAN). Its annotation score (1 to 5) says how much is known about it, not how correct it is.

Search

Type a protein name, a gene name, an accession or any words, and Search. Tick Reviewed (Swiss-Prot) only and Human (Homo sapiens) only to narrow the list. An accession on its own opens the entry. The results come 25 at a time (Show 25 more); click an accession to open it.

UniProt's query language works in the box:

QueryFinds
gene:CYP2C19 AND organism_id:9606the human entries of a gene
reviewed:true AND length:[100 TO 120]reviewed proteins of 100 to 120 amino acids
ec:3.4.21.4proteins with that enzyme activity (EC number)
keyword:KW-0675 AND organism_id:9606human proteins with the keyword Receptor
cc_disease:Alzheimer AND reviewed:truereviewed entries that mention the disease

An entry

The top of an entry gives the protein's name, gene, organism, status, annotation score and length, with buttons: AlphaFold model in 3D, Save FASTA (to your files) and Send to notebook (Python or R code that fetches the entry). The sections are listed on the left:

SectionWhat it says
Functionwhat the protein does, catalytic activity, GO terms
Names & Taxonomythe names of the protein and gene, the organism and its lineage
Subcellular Locationwhere in the cell, and the membrane topology
Disease & Variantsdiseases, natural variants, mutagenesis experiments
PTM / Processingsignal peptides, chains, modified residues, disulfide bonds, glycosylation
Expressiontissues and developmental stages
Interactionsubunits and binary interactions (IntAct); the protein's STRING network or its IntAct interactions in the Networks tab
Structurethe PDB entries (which parts of the protein each covers) and the AlphaFold model
Family & Domainsdomains, regions, motifs, repeats, and the family
Sequence & Isoformsthe sequence – find a motif, go to a residue, copy or save it as FASTA – and how the other isoforms differ

Many statements carry an evidence tag: experimental evidence, or inferred by similarity to another protein (ECO codes). Positions are numbered along the canonical sequence of the entry.

Features in 3D

Domains, sites, variants and other features are drawn along the sequence. Click one to see where it is in the 3D viewer: the positions are found on the structure that is loaded – a PDB entry of this protein, or its AlphaFold model – by aligning its sequence with the entry's. If no structure of the protein is loaded, or the residues are not in it (experimental structures often contain only part of a protein), it offers to load the AlphaFold model, which covers the whole sequence.

In Structure, the coverage map shows which residues each PDB entry contains; click a bar, or 3D in the table, to load that structure, and Details to open it in the Protein Data Bank card.

From code

An entry as JSON is https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P04637.json, its sequence …/P04637.fasta, and a search https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/search?query=…. Send to notebook writes the code; or:

import requests
import pandas as pd

r = requests.get("https://rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/search",
                 params={"query": "gene:CYP2C19 AND reviewed:true", "fields": "accession,id,organism_name,length", "format": "tsv"})
from io import StringIO
pd.read_csv(StringIO(r.text), sep="\t")
